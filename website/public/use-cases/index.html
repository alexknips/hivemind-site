Use cases and roadmap

Point your coding agent at HiveMind,
and it records the why as it works.

Concrete ways one engineer and their agents put HiveMind to work: what works today, what is coming next, and what is planned. Nothing here is a mock-up; every card for today shows the actual command or tool call.

What works today

v0.7.0

Every write says who made it and is never edited or deleted. Status (proposed, accepted, contested, superseded) follows from the record, never from a label somebody sets by hand.

Works today

Record the decision as you work

Agent
Mid-task, your agent records the choice, the options it weighed and why, and what the decision rests on, without leaving the session. It records who decided: you, or the agent.

capture_decision(title, rationale, options, chosen_option_label, grounding, decided_by)
Works today

Ask "why did we choose this?"

Human · Agent
In a fresh session, with no chat history: the rationale, the options chosen and rejected, who decided, and whether it still holds. Describe the decision; no id needed.

hivemind query why "sqlite for local storage"
Works today

Ask "did it hold up?"

Human · Agent
Whether the decision still stands, or was replaced, disputed, or rests on a premise that no longer holds. With the reasons, from the record.

hivemind query verify "sqlite for local storage"
Works today

Disagreement stays on the record

Human · Agent
Push back on a decision and both sides are kept. It reads contested, a state you can ask about, not a lost thread.

hivemind disagree "sqlite for local storage" --reason …
Works today

Replace without erasing

Human · Agent
Record the new decision as replacing the old one. The old one reads superseded and says what replaced it; the whole chain stays, walkable back to the first proposal.

hivemind supersede "sqlite for local storage" --title … --rationale …

Every decision says what it rests on

Human · Agent
An earlier decision, something observed, an assumption, or a bet with a date to check. When that premise is later replaced or refuted, the decisions built on it read stale. A capture that names nothing is refused.

hivemind emit decision.capture … --rests-on-decision "ids never change"

Several projects, one record

Human · Agent
Every decision carries its project. A question asked from one project also looks in the project it is part of and the ones it depends on, one hop, and says where it stopped looking.

hivemind query situational --project mobile-client

Look over a week of agent decisions

Human
What your agents decided this week, one by one: accept, dispute or replace each, in the terminal, without watching them live.

hivemind review --actor 'agent:*' --since 7d
Works today

The decision log as Markdown

Human
Export every decision as a tree of Markdown files grouped by project, an index first. Readable without HiveMind, and it can live in your repo.

hivemind export --format markdown --out ./decisions/
Works today

Turn existing notes into decisions

Human
Import the Decision: blocks from your docs. Each becomes a decision carrying its source, and lands as unreviewed until you look at it.

hivemind import documents ./notes/
Works today · experimental

Import decisions from living documents

Human
Pull decision candidates from a Google Doc or a file in git history. Each import records the source at the revision it read, so the citation stays honest as the document moves. Google Docs only; Confluence is not supported yet.

hivemind import connector --url <gdocs-url>

Built, not yet released, or under way

Merged and waiting for the next release, or being built now. Shown as intent until it is in a release you can install.

Coming next

A much simpler, decision-first UI

A list of decisions, newest first, as the front door, scoped by project. One decision's neighbourhood as the graph, with every arrow running from newer to older. Less chrome.

Coming next

The plugin asks what a decision rests on

In v0.7.0 the capture plugin does not ask up front; when your agent leaves it out, HiveMind refuses the capture and names the four ways to answer. The updated plugin asks the question first.

Coming next

How well made was this decision?

A profile on seven dimensions: framing, alternatives, information, reasoning, values and trade-offs, bias exposure, calibration. Each line names the recorded facts it was read from, and says "not assessed" where there is nothing to read. Not a grade, and never a mark against changing your mind.

Coming next

Capture that runs all day

Your sessions with your agents are read as they happen and the decisions in them are recorded, with their options, reasons and who decided, so nobody has to say "record this". The pieces are in the current release; the setup that makes it one step is not.

Planned

Decided, not built

On the roadmap, in the open. Shown here as intent, not as something that exists yet.

Planned

A disagreement travels

When a decision is replaced or disputed, every decision resting on it gets an open re-examine item, closed only by an explicit "still holds" or by its own replacement. The new decision shows how many of them are done, so a half-finished reversal is visible instead of quietly forgotten.

Planned

Two tracks of your work, quietly diverging

Your agents work the API and the mobile client in parallel, and one replaces a decision the other is building on. Today, a decision that rests on a replaced premise already reads stale, also across linked projects. Planned: catching two tracks that contradict each other with no recorded link between them.

Planned

Speed, consistency and flexibility

Across your decisions with your agents: how fast you get from question to decision, how often a new decision stays in line with standing ones, and how often you change your mind when evidence arrives. Shown side by side, never as one number, never as a ranking. A fast reversal on new evidence reads as a strength.

Planned

With and without HiveMind, side by side

The same engineering task run twice by a coding agent: once with a hand-kept decisions file, once with HiveMind. Recorded, reproducible, and the site will say what it shows and no more.

Planned

The demo becomes the app

The demo on this site becomes the app itself, read-only, over example data built for it, labelled as such on screen. The jury room of 12 Angry Men stays as one of the projects to explore.
