The problem

Your agent forgets
what you decided.

  • It undid itYou dropped the ORM on purpose. A few weeks later the agent helpfully brings it back, with confidence.
  • Nobody knows whygit blame says who changed a line, not why the agent did it that way. The chat was the design meeting, and it is gone.
  • It went staleThe DECISIONS.md you keep by hand misses a reversal, and the agent follows the dead entry.

What it is

HiveMind is memory
for decisions.

Your coding agents record what was decided, which options lost and why, and who decided, you or the agent. The next session does not reintroduce what you dropped or rewrite what you agreed. When a decision is contested or replaced, its status changes; nothing goes quietly stale.

One engineer and their agents, local-first, AGPL-3.0, free to self-host.

What you get

Memory, but for decisions.

When an agent forgets, you look for a fix under memory. HiveMind keeps two things that memory tools don't.

The why

The options that lost and the reasons, the evidence, and who decided: you or an agent. Ask later, and the answer comes from the record.

Whether it held up

When a decision is contested or replaced, its status says so. Nobody has to remember to update it, and the old entry is never edited or deleted.

It's yours: one binary and a SQLite ledger in your project. No account, no sign-up.

Where it is

Early, and self-hosted.

  • Works todayClaude Code, Codex and Cursor connect over MCP. Your agent records decisions with the options, the reasons and who decided, and answers why and whether it held up. There's a CLI for the same.
  • Runs onOne binary for Linux and Apple Silicon Macs, with a SQLite ledger in your project. AGPL-3.0. No hosted version.
  • To be fairThe screens are from main. In the v0.6.0 release, a decision your agent records stays “proposed”, it can't record you as the one who decided, and “did it hold up?” returns an error. The fixes are on main and go out with the next release.
  • NextA much simpler, decision-first UI. The redesign is under way.
  • ThenThe same agent work with and without HiveMind, side by side, to show whether it helps.
